# Which preposition to use with time

- **Days & dates → _on_**
    
    - on Monday / on Friday / on June 5 / on my birthday
        
    - _I’ll see you **on** Tuesday._
        
- **Months, years, seasons, centuries, decades → _in_**
    
    - in July / in 2024 / in winter / in the 1990s
        
    - _She was born **in** 1998._
        
- **Exact clock times & certain fixed moments → _at_**
    
    - at 7:00, at 7 pm, at noon, at midnight, at lunchtime, at dawn
        
    - _The meeting starts **at** 3:30 pm._
        
- **Parts of the day → usually _in_ (except night)**
    
    - in the morning / in the afternoon / in the evening
        
    - but **at night** (we say _at night_, not _in the night_)
        
    - _I exercise **in** the morning._
        
- **Weekend — regional difference**
    
    - UK: _at the weekend_ (common)
        
    - US: _on the weekend_ (common)
        
    - _I’m busy **this weekend**._ (no preposition when you say “this/last/next weekend”)
        
- **No preposition in routine schedules sometimes**
    
    - _I work Monday to Friday._ (schedule — no “on” required)
        
- **Other useful prepositions with time**
    
    - **by** = not later than (deadline): _Please finish it **by** 5pm._
        
    - **until / till / to** = up to a point: _I’ll wait **until** 6._
        
    - **from … to/until** = time range: _Open **from** 9 **to** 5._
        
    - **for** = duration: _I worked there **for** two years._
        
    - **since** = start point to now: _I’ve lived here **since** 2019._
        
- **On time vs. in time**
    
    - **on time** = punctual (exactly when expected): _The train arrived **on time**._
        
    - **in time** = before a deadline or before something happens: _We got there **in time** to see the show._
